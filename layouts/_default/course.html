Curriculum

Four modules that build on each other.

Start with the fundamentals. Finish with a portfolio plan you can act on.

  1. 01

    Real Estate 101

    Understand the language, process, and systems that drive every smart real estate decision. We start from zero — terminology, market dynamics, deal structures — so the rest of the course builds on solid ground.

    • How real estate actually makes money (and how it loses it)
    • Buyer/seller dynamics, agents, attorneys, and lenders explained
    • Reading MLS listings, public records, and tax data
    • Mistakes that wipe out first-time investors
  2. 02

    Property Analysis & Comps

    Evaluate any property, run the numbers, and decide if the deal is worth pursuing — without depending on a realtor or “feel.” This is where most courses get vague. We get specific.

    • The exact spreadsheet Farrah uses on every deal
    • Running comps in any market — including markets you’ve never visited
    • Estimating rehab costs without a contractor walkthrough
    • Cap rate, cash-on-cash, IRR — when each one matters
  3. 03

    Portfolio Strategy

    Long-term, mid-term, short-term, and hybrid rentals — each works in different conditions. Pick the right strategy for your goals, market, and risk tolerance, then build a portfolio around it.

    • How to choose between LTR, MTR, STR, and house-hacking
    • Funding strategies — conventional, DSCR, hard money, partnerships
    • Building a portfolio that compounds without burning you out
    • Tax-aware structuring (LLCs, holding companies, the basics)
  4. 04

    Financial Freedom Planning

    Real estate is a means, not an end. This module is the planning layer — translating properties into a clear retirement timeline, an income replacement number, and a roadmap that actually fits your life.

    • Calculate your real “freedom number” — what income do you actually need?
    • Build a 5- to 15-year acquisition plan
    • Insurance, estate planning, and protecting what you build
    • Mindset work — the part most courses skip, and why it matters
